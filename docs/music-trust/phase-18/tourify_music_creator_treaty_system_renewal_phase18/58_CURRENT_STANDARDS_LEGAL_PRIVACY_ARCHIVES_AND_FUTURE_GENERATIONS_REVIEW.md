# Current Standards, Legal, Privacy, Archives and Future-Generations Review

**Research date:** 2026-07-19  
**Status:** architecture research only; not legal advice and not evidence that any legal instrument or organization exists.

## 1. Future generations and long-term governance

The United Nations General Assembly adopted the Pact for the Future, including the Declaration on Future Generations, on 22 September 2024 as A/RES/79/1. The Declaration recognizes intergenerational solidarity, justice and equity and calls for strategic foresight, forward-looking impact assessment, anticipatory risk analysis, stakeholder cooperation and institutionalized long-term thinking. Phase 18 uses those concepts as public-governance references but does not claim UN affiliation or authority.

Primary sources:
- https://www.un.org/pact-for-the-future/en/annex-ii-declaration-future-generations
- https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4061879
- https://www.un.org/pact-for-the-future/en/paragraph-31

Implementation implications:
- create future-generation impact assessments for major renewal, termination and asset decisions;
- use multiple time horizons and plausible scenarios rather than one forecast;
- publish assumptions, uncertainty, distributional effects and review triggers;
- provide youth and intergenerational participation without inventing legal representation.

## 2. Ex post review, sunset and renewal

OECD best-practice work treats ex post review as a permanent part of the regulatory cycle and identifies programmed reviews, post-implementation review and sunset requirements as useful tools. It also warns that sunset mechanisms require careful governance and independent, evidence-based review. Phase 18 therefore makes renewal affirmative and treats silence as expiry rather than approval.

Primary sources:
- https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/reviewing-the-stock-of-regulation_1a8f33bc-en.html
- https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/reviewing-the-stock-of-regulation_1a8f33bc-en/full-report/component-8.html
- https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/measuring-regulatory-performance.html

Implementation implications:
- every renewable service records `review_due_at` and `sunset_at`;
- renewal requires a new decision and effective period;
- independent review is required for high-impact or sensitive powers;
- safe continuity and archival access remain available after expiry while new high-impact actions are denied.

## 3. Documentary heritage, records and archives

UNESCO's 2015 Recommendation concerning documentary heritage, including digital form, emphasizes ongoing preservation, integrity, authenticity, accessibility, risk management, international cooperation and use of standardized interfaces. It also recognizes that access restrictions may remain necessary for privacy, safety, security and confidentiality.

Primary sources:
- https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-concerning-preservation-and-access-documentary-heritage-including-digital-form
- https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/monitoring/3rd-consultation-2027-0

The UN Archives records-management guidance treats records as evidence of activities and transactions and manages them through creation, maintenance, disposition and archival preservation. Its digital-preservation guidance emphasizes authenticity, accessibility and usability over time.

Primary sources:
- https://archives.un.org/en/content/manage-records/policy
- https://archives.un.org/en/content/digital-preservation
- https://archives.un.org/en/content/manage-records/dispose

Implementation implications:
- create approved retention and disposition schedules;
- preserve restricted evidence even when public access is limited;
- use fixity, provenance, format and custody metadata;
- test independent restoration, legal holds and documented destruction.

## 4. OAIS and long-term digital preservation

ISO 14721:2025 is the current third edition of the Open Archival Information System reference model. It defines an archive as an organization, people and systems accepting responsibility to preserve information and make it available to a designated community, and addresses ingest, archival storage, data management, access, migration and representation information.

Primary source:
- https://www.iso.org/standard/87471.html

Implementation implications:
- define designated communities and representation-information needs;
- package provenance, fixity, context, rights and format information with archival objects;
- preserve the ability to understand and verify historical records even when technology changes;
- do not equate “open” with unrestricted access.

## 5. Treaty lifecycle, succession and termination boundaries

The Vienna Convention materials and International Law Commission resources distinguish amendment, modification, suspension, termination and succession. The 1986 Vienna Convention materials also address treaties involving international organizations and note questions arising from termination of an organization. Phase 18 models these as separate legal states and does not infer continuity from software records.

Primary sources:
- https://legal.un.org/diplconf_records/1986_lot/
- https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/3_2.shtml
- https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/3_3.shtml
- https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/1_2.shtml

Implementation implications:
- succession evidence does not itself prove inherited authority;
- preserve authentic instruments, notifications, reservations and effective dates;
- distinguish institution, participant, protocol, archive and asset succession;
- require current qualified legal review for actual public-law effects.

## 6. Current implementation cautions

- Do not label a future-generation advisory body as a legal representative for unborn persons.
- Do not make an institution perpetual because its database, domain or operators remain available.
- Do not destroy restricted evidence merely because public access is revoked.
- Do not preserve personal data indefinitely without a lawful retention or archival basis.
- Do not rely on current cryptography, file formats, cloud vendors or identifier methods as permanent.
- Do not rewrite historical records to current schemas without preserving the original representation and transformation evidence.
- Do not allow emergency continuity powers to become permanent authority.
