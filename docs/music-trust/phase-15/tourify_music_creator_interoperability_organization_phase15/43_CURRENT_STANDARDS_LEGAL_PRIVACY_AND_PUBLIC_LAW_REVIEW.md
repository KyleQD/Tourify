# Current Standards, Legal, Privacy and Public-Law Review

## Research date

July 18, 2026. This is implementation research, not legal advice. Counsel must confirm all current law, state practice, authority, host-country rules, tax, employment, privacy, privileges, and institutional arrangements before production.

## Legal personality and constitutive instruments

- The UN Charter provides that the United Nations enjoys legal capacity necessary for its functions and privileges and immunities necessary for its purposes. These are functions of the Charter and implementing conventions, not software labels: https://legal.un.org/repertory/art104_105.shtml
- The WIPO Convention expressly addresses establishment, membership, organs, headquarters, finances, legal capacity, privileges and immunities, relations with other organizations, entry into force, amendments, denunciation, and notifications: https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/283854
- The ILO Constitution provides full juridical personality and capacities to contract, hold property, and institute legal proceedings; it also links privileges and immunities to functional necessity: https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=1000:62:::::P62_LIST_ENTRIE_ID:2453907:NO

**Implementation consequence:** Phase 15 must not claim international legal personality until a valid constitutive instrument and applicable participating-state law establish it. Domestic legal personality, international legal personality, treaty capacity, privileges, and regulatory power are separately evaluated.

## United Nations relationship and specialized-agency status

- UN Charter Article 57 describes specialized agencies as established by intergovernmental agreement with wide international responsibilities and brought into relationship with the UN. Article 63 provides for ECOSOC relationship agreements subject to General Assembly approval: https://legal.un.org/repertory/art57.shtml and https://legal.un.org/repertory/art63.shtml

**Implementation consequence:** no UN, specialized-agency, diplomatic, or endorsement label is enabled from Phase 15 records. A relationship agreement would require a later, externally governed process.

## Privileges, immunities and headquarters agreements

- The Convention on the Privileges and Immunities of the Specialized Agencies is a multilateral instrument whose application depends on state participation and the relevant agency annex: https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?chapter=3&mtdsg_no=III-2&src=TREATY
- WIPO's constitutive framework contemplates headquarters and other agreements for legal capacity, privileges and immunities: https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/fr/text/500546
- WIPO maintains an official headquarters agreement with Switzerland: https://www.wipo.int/meetings/en/doc_details.jsp?doc_id=635374

**Implementation consequence:** privileges and immunities remain `not_applicable` or `proposed` until an effective legal instrument identifies beneficiaries, territory, scope, waiver, and alternative remedies. The UI must not use diplomatic language prematurely.

## Depositary, authentic texts and treaty practice

- The UN Treaty Handbook and Final Clauses Handbook describe the Secretary-General's depositary, registration, publication, instrument, reservation, notification, authentic-text, and final-clause practice: https://treaties.un.org/Pages/Resource.aspx?path=Publication/TH/Page1_en.xml and https://treaties.un.org/Pages/Resource.aspx?path=Publication/FC/Page1_en.xml
- The Secretary-General is depositary for hundreds of multilateral treaties and the Treaty Collection maintains current status and depositary notifications: https://treaties.un.org/
- The 1986 Vienna Convention addresses treaties involving international organizations but its status and application must be checked for the relevant parties and issue: https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?chapter=23&mtdsg_no=XXIII-3&src=TREATY

**Implementation consequence:** Phase 15 initially uses private instrument custody. Formal depositary status is recorded only when designated by a valid instrument and accepted by the responsible entity.

## Responsibility and accountability

- The International Law Commission adopted draft Articles on the Responsibility of International Organizations in 2011. They address wrongful acts, attribution, responsibility, implementation, and state connections, but are not a substitute for matter-specific law: https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/9_11.shtml
- The UN Office of Internal Oversight Services separates internal audit, investigation, inspection, and evaluation and operates with functional independence: https://oios.un.org/en
- The Joint Inspection Unit's 2023 review examines accountability frameworks across UN-system organizations: https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4013015

**Implementation consequence:** governing bodies, management, internal audit, external audit, investigations, ethics, evaluation, and independent review are separate capabilities with protected access and reporting.

## Staff justice and ethics

- The ILO Administrative Tribunal can hear employment complaints from organizations that validly recognize its jurisdiction and meet its requirements: https://www.ilo.org/resource/statute-administrative-tribunal-international-labour-organization
- The UN Ethics Office separates ethics advice, financial disclosure, training, and protection against retaliation: https://www.un.org/en/ethics/

**Implementation consequence:** immunity cannot be used to leave staff without reasonable access to justice. Phase 15 must select and fund an employment-dispute system before asserting any jurisdictional immunity.

## Funding and independence

- International organizations use different mixes of assessed contributions, voluntary core funds, earmarked funding, and service income. UNDP, for example, relies on voluntary contributions and distinguishes flexible core from earmarked resources: https://www.undp.org/funding

**Implementation consequence:** Tourify must model funding concentration, donor conditions, arrears, reserves, assessed contributions, voluntary resources, procurement influence, and conflicts separately. No donor purchases votes, recognition, standards outcomes, or enforcement decisions.

## Standards and institutional precedents

- ITU's Constitution and Convention form the treaty basis for the Union and its institutional structure: https://www.itu.int/en/history/pages/constitutionandconvention.aspx
- WIPO's convention and operations demonstrate a model combining treaty-based governance, technical services, standards, member contributions, fee income, legal capacity, and headquarters arrangements: https://www.wipo.int/en/web/treaties/convention/summary_wipo_convention

**Implementation consequence:** these are comparative precedents only. Tourify may not copy names, status, legal powers, marks, or privileges.

## Required recurring verification

Before any implementation or activation, verify current treaty status, participating jurisdictions, applicable host-country law, privacy and data-transfer law, sanctions and export controls, competition rules, employment jurisdiction, tax, privileges, standards versions, provider contracts, and institutional approvals.
