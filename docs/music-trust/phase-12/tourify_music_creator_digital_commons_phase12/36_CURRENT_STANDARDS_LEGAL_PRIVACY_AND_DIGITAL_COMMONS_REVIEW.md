# Current Standards, Legal, Privacy and Digital Commons Review

**Review date:** 2026-07-17

This document is an implementation research baseline, not legal advice or an endorsement by any cited organization. Codex must re-check official sources before production decisions.

## Status discipline

- **Recommendation / final standard:** suitable for a pinned implementation profile after security and interoperability review.
- **Working Draft / Candidate / Community Report:** informative or experimental; do not represent as a stable standard.
- **Governance example:** useful precedent, not a legal template or automatic fit for Tourify.
- **Public-interest framework:** design guidance, not certification or approval.

## Digital public infrastructure and safeguards

UNDP describes digital public infrastructure as shared infrastructure requiring governance, funding and institutional responsibilities in addition to software. Its Universal DPI Safeguards work emphasizes safety, inclusion and structural-vulnerability controls across the lifecycle.

- [UNDP Digital Public Infrastructure](https://www.undp.org/digital/digital-public-infrastructure)
- [UNDP governance assessment for data exchange systems, November 3, 2025](https://www.undp.org/digital/blog/building-fairer-digital-future-starts-trusted-data-governance-assessment-framework-digital-public-infrastructure)
- [UNDP and DPGA safeguards alignment](https://www.undp.org/digital/blog/how-dpg-standard-and-universal-dpi-safeguards-framework-are-charting-safe-and-inclusive-digital-future)
- [World Bank Global DPI Program, May 6, 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2026/05/06/global-digital-public-infrastructure-program)

Implementation consequence: Phase 12 must treat governance, funding, legal responsibility, accessibility, redress, security and operator independence as production dependencies rather than documentation afterthoughts.

## Global Digital Compact

The UN Global Digital Compact calls for multistakeholder cooperation, safe and interoperable DPI, open standards, digital public goods and investment in public-interest infrastructure. It also recognizes that multiple models will reflect local priorities.

- [Global Digital Compact implementation site](https://www.un.org/global-digital-compact/en)
- [Pact for the Future, Annex I: Global Digital Compact](https://www.un.org/pact-for-the-future/en/annex-i-global-digital-compact)

Implementation consequence: Phase 12 should support interoperability and local sovereignty rather than enforce one universal creator identity or governance model.

## Digital Public Goods Standard

The Digital Public Goods Standard currently identifies nine major indicators including open licensing, clear ownership, platform independence, documentation, privacy, open standards and security. Recognition applies to the core solution rather than every local implementation.

- [Digital Public Goods Standard](https://www.digitalpublicgoods.net/standard)

Implementation consequence: Tourify may use the standard as a readiness benchmark but must not claim recognition without completing the DPGA review. Open licensing alone does not establish public benefit, safe governance or local compliance.

## Neutral stewardship and community assets

The IETF Trust is an example of a separate legal vehicle that holds intellectual property used in the Internet standards process. It also assumed stewardship of IANA-related domain names and trademarks through a transition designed to keep those assets independent of the functions operator.

- [IETF Trust](https://www.ietf.org/about/groups/trust/)
- [IANA Intellectual Property stewardship](https://trustee.ietf.org/iana-ipr/)
- [IETF Trust assets](https://trustee.ietf.org/assets/)

The Linux Foundation describes neutral ownership of community assets such as trademarks, repositories and domains as a component of open governance and provider-neutral project hosting.

- [Linux Foundation project hosting](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/projects/hosting)
- [Linux Foundation on neutral trademark control](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/blog/blog/open-source-communities-and-trademarks-a-reprise)

Implementation consequence: Phase 12 must maintain a verified asset register and distinguish legal ownership, neutral stewardship, custody and operational access. These examples do not determine the correct Tourify entity or transfer structure.

## Accountability and multistakeholder governance

ICANN documents formal accountability mechanisms intended to support its bottom-up multistakeholder model. The example is useful for review, reconsideration and transparency design, but creator infrastructure has different legal and rights consequences.

- [ICANN accountability mechanisms](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/mechanisms-2014-03-20-en)

Implementation consequence: Commons decisions need published authority, rationale, conflicts, appeal and remedy. Tourify must not copy governance structures without creator-specific power analysis.

## Credentials and trust

W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0, Data Integrity, Controlled Identifiers and Bitstring Status List became W3C Recommendations on May 15, 2025. The VC Working Group is developing 2.1 and recognition-related drafts in 2026.

- [W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 Recommendations](https://www.w3.org/news/2025/the-verifiable-credentials-2-0-family-of-specifications-is-now-a-w3c-recommendation/)
- [W3C VC publications](https://www.w3.org/groups/wg/vc/publications/)
- [Recognized Entities v1.0 First Public Working Draft, May 12, 2026](https://www.w3.org/news/2026/first-public-working-draft-recognized-entities-v1-0/)

Implementation consequence: pin the stable 2.0 family unless an approved profile deliberately adopts a later draft. Credentials are signed claims and must not replace current mandate, rights or registry authorization checks.

## Open protocol and software commons

Phase 12 should separately govern:

- specification text and schemas;
- source code and reference implementations;
- conformance suites and marks;
- domains and package namespaces;
- trademarks and certification marks;
- public registry projections;
- cryptographic roots and status services;
- documentation, translations and accessibility assets.

Each asset needs a documented owner or steward, licence, contribution path, change process, dependency map, operator, backup and transition plan.

## Required legal and professional reviews

Before production, obtain written review covering at least:

1. steward entity and fiduciary duties;
2. asset ownership, transfer, escrow and trademark control;
3. software, specification, data and content licensing;
4. privacy, public records, retention and cross-border transfers;
5. competition and collective-action boundaries;
6. accessibility and nondiscrimination obligations;
7. procurement, grants, tax and public-benefit claims;
8. cybersecurity, incident notification and key custody;
9. contract step-in, insolvency and operator replacement; and
10. jurisdiction-specific registry and identity rules.

## Re-review triggers

Refresh this review when a relevant W3C specification changes status, the DPG Standard changes, a steward entity or operator is selected, assets are proposed for transfer, new jurisdictions activate, a public API expands, or a security/privacy incident changes the threat model.
