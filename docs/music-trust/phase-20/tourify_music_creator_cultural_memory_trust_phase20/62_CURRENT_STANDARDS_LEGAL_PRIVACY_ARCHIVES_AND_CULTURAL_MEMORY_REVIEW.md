# Current standards, legal, privacy, archives and cultural-memory review

Reviewed: 2026-07-20

## Status and use

This is a planning research matrix, not legal advice, certification, treaty recognition or professional archival approval. Codex must re-check every source before implementation because standards, law, guidance, participation status and organizational policies change.

## Digital preservation and trustworthy repositories

### ISO 14721:2025 — OAIS

- Official source: https://www.iso.org/standard/87471.html
- Status: published March 2025, third edition.
- Relevance: defines an Open Archival Information System, mandatory archive responsibilities, designated communities, information models, migration and archive exchange.
- Constraint: “open” does not mean unrestricted access. Phase 20 may profile OAIS concepts but must not claim conformance or certification without a scoped assessment.

### ISO 16363:2025 — trustworthy repository audit and certification

- Official source: https://www.iso.org/standard/87472.html
- Status: published March 2025, second edition.
- Relevance: audit and certification basis for trustworthy digital repositories.
- Constraint: self-assessment, external audit and formal certification must be labeled separately.

### PREMIS 3.0

- Official source: https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/v3/index.html
- Relevance: preservation metadata for Objects, Events, Rights and Agents; useful for provenance, preservation actions and rights restrictions.
- Constraint: PREMIS is a preservation metadata standard, not a copyright registry or ownership adjudicator.

### BagIt RFC 8493

- Official source: https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8493/
- Status: Informational RFC, not an Internet Standards Track specification.
- Relevance: reliable packaging and transfer using manifests and checksums.
- Constraint: use an explicit Phase 20 profile and stronger current checksum policy; BagIt validation proves package completeness and checksum matching, not legal authenticity or truth.

### Library of Congress format guidance

- Sustainability factors: https://www.loc.gov/preservation/digital/formats/
- Recommended Formats Statement 2025–2026: https://www.loc.gov/preservation/resources/rfs/
- Relevance: format transparency, adoption, self-documentation, dependencies, patents, technical protection and content-specific quality.
- Constraint: format preferences are collection and purpose dependent; preserve originals and record migration loss.

## Documentary and intangible cultural heritage

### UNESCO 2015 Recommendation on documentary heritage

- Official source: https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-concerning-preservation-and-access-documentary-heritage-including-digital-form
- Relevance: integrity, authenticity, reliability, memory institutions, international cooperation, standards, access and legitimate restrictions.
- Important boundary: legitimate access restrictions should not prevent preservation; restrictions should be defined, supported by law or policy and include appeal where appropriate.

### UNESCO Memory of the World Programme

- Official source: https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/about
- Current programme description updated February 2026.
- Relevance: preservation, access and awareness through national and regional committees.
- Constraint: Phase 20 does not claim UNESCO recognition or Memory of the World registration.

### 2003 Convention for the Safeguarding of Intangible Cultural Heritage

- Official source: https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/convention-safeguarding-intangible-cultural-heritage
- Relevance: safeguarding and respect for communities, groups and individuals concerned.
- 2024 basic texts: https://ich.unesco.org/en/basic-texts-00503

### UNESCO Ethical Principles for Intangible Cultural Heritage

- Official source: https://ich.unesco.org/en/ethics-and-ich-00866
- Relevance: communities should have the primary safeguarding role; interactions should use transparent collaboration and free, prior, sustained and informed consent; customary access restrictions should be respected.
- Constraint: applicability and legal effect require jurisdiction and community-specific review.

### 2005 Convention on Diversity of Cultural Expressions

- Official source: https://www.unesco.org/creativity/en/2005-convention
- Relevance: diversity of cultural expressions, civil-society participation and cultural policy.
- Constraint: it does not authorize Phase 20 to represent cultures or creators.

## Indigenous Peoples, communities and traditional knowledge

### United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples

- Official source: https://www.un.org/development/desa/indigenouspeoples/declaration-on-the-rights-of-indigenous-peoples.html
- Relevance: self-determination, identity, participation, cultural heritage, traditional knowledge and language.
- Constraint: Phase 20 must not determine Indigenous identity or authority through automated inference.

### WIPO Treaty on Intellectual Property, Genetic Resources and Associated Traditional Knowledge

- Official source: https://www.wipo.int/en/web/treaties/ip/gratk/index
- Adopted May 24, 2024; entry into force depends on the treaty’s ratification/accession threshold.
- Relevance: demonstrates the need to distinguish intellectual-property systems, genetic resources, traditional knowledge, Indigenous Peoples and local communities.
- Constraint: it concerns patent disclosure and does not create a general archive-access or music-rights regime.

## Return, restitution and cultural property

### UNESCO 1970 Convention

- Official source: https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/convention-means-prohibiting-and-preventing-illicit-import-export-and-transfer-ownership-cultural
- Relevance: illicit import/export, provenance, return and restitution, cooperation and adequate heritage services.
- Constraint: digital return, shared custody and music archives require separate legal and ethical analysis; the Convention does not automatically resolve all historic claims.

### ICOM Code of Ethics

- Official source: https://icom.museum/en/resources/standards-guidelines/code-of-ethics/
- Relevance: provenance, due diligence, returns, restitution, professional ethics and conflicts.
- Constraint: professional code, not a substitute for applicable law or community authority.

## Entity and funding options

### Nevada nonprofit corporations

- Official source: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-082.html
- Relevance: Nevada nonprofit and public-benefit corporation framework, including charitable-asset oversight.
- Constraint: counsel must assess whether Nevada is appropriate for a globally plural trust and how foreign qualification, charitable solicitation and fiduciary law apply.

### Nevada trusts

- Official source: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-163.html
- Relevance: trust creation, trust property, fiduciary duties and public-benefit or purpose trust options.
- Constraint: “cultural-memory trust” is a product/governance term until a valid trust instrument and trust property exist.

### U.S. federal tax exemption

- Exempt purposes: https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/exempt-purposes-internal-revenue-code-section-501c3
- Public charity/private foundation distinction: https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/eo-operational-requirements-private-foundations-and-public-charities
- Relevance: educational, scientific, literary and charitable purposes may be relevant; foundation classification affects governance and restrictions.
- Constraint: no tax-exempt status may be claimed before formation, application and IRS determination where required.

## Privacy and research

### European Data Protection Board Guidelines 1/2026

- Official consultation source: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/public-consultations/guidelines-12026-on-processing-of-personal-data-for-scientific-research_en
- Status: public-consultation guidelines in 2026, not treated here as final binding law.
- Relevance: lawful processing, controller/processor roles, data-subject rights and research safeguards.
- Constraint: archival, research, public-interest and erasure questions require jurisdiction-specific legal analysis.

## Required implementation conclusions

1. Do not claim certification, UNESCO recognition, treaty status, trust status or tax exemption by reference to a standard or draft.
2. Use multiple independent custodians and operators.
3. Preserve original records, provenance, restrictions and source authority.
4. Separate preservation from disclosure and reuse.
5. Require community and creator authority for cultural restrictions and reuse where applicable.
6. Keep public finding aids minimal and non-authorizing.
7. Require separate legal review for entity formation, charitable status, privacy, copyright, repatriation, cross-border transfer and cultural authority.
8. Re-run this research review immediately before any public pilot.
