# Current Standards, Legal, Privacy and Constitutional Stewardship Review

**Research date:** July 17, 2026

This document is a design reference, not legal advice and not evidence that any standards body, government or public-interest organization endorses Tourify. Counsel and standards specialists must refresh every time-sensitive source before implementation.

## 1. W3C process and appeals

The operative W3C Process Document is dated August 18, 2025. It provides useful precedents for charter review, consensus, recorded dissent, Formal Objections, Councils, appeals and public documentation. Phase 13 borrows the ideas of effect-based change classification, reasoned objection handling and independent review, but does not claim to be W3C or to reproduce its institutional authority.

Primary sources:
- https://www.w3.org/policies/process/
- https://www.w3.org/news/2025/w3c-updates-its-process-document/

## 2. IETF process and neutral intellectual-property stewardship

IETF BCP 9 documents an open Internet standards process. The IETF Trust was formed to hold and license intellectual property used in the Internet standards process, and in 2016 accepted stewardship of IANA-related domains and trademarks independently from the IANA functions operator. This is a strong precedent for separating protocol operation from custody of critical names, marks and documents.

Primary sources:
- https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc2026/
- https://trustee.ietf.org/documents/trust-legal-provisions/
- https://trustee.ietf.org/iana-ipr/
- https://trustee.ietf.org/assets/

## 3. ICANN/IANA accountability and succession

The IANA stewardship transition separated operational functions, intellectual-property custody, service oversight and community accountability. ICANN’s Empowered Community currently has powers involving budgets, fundamental bylaws, asset sales, board removal and independent review. Phase 13 uses this as a design precedent for entrenched provisions, high-threshold changes, community powers, operator oversight and succession—not as a template to copy without independent legal analysis.

Primary sources:
- https://www.icann.org/en/ec
- https://www.icann.org/en/stewardship-implementation
- https://www.icann.org/iana-stewardship-questions
- https://www.iana.org/performance/csc-reports
- https://www.iana.org/help/complaint-procedure

## 4. United Nations Global Digital Compact

The Global Digital Compact, adopted in 2024, supports an open, global, interoperable, stable and secure Internet; multi-stakeholder participation; open standards; digital public goods; inclusive digital public infrastructure; safeguards; and interoperable data governance. Phase 13 should align with these public-interest principles while avoiding any suggestion that its compact is a treaty or intergovernmental instrument.

Primary sources:
- https://www.un.org/pact-for-the-future/en/annex-i-global-digital-compact
- https://www.un.org/global-digital-compact/en

## 5. DPI safeguards and governance assessment

UNDP’s Universal DPI Safeguards work and 2025 governance-assessment materials emphasize rights-based safeguards, independent oversight, accountability, inclusion and lifecycle governance. These support Phase 13 requirements for human review, appeals, accessibility, incident remedy and independent oversight.

Primary sources:
- https://www.undp.org/digital/digital-public-infrastructure
- https://www.undp.org/press-releases/un-releases-universal-dpi-safeguards-framework-promote-safe-and-inclusive-digital-public-infrastructure
- https://www.undp.org/digital/blog/building-fairer-digital-future-starts-trusted-data-governance-assessment-framework-digital-public-infrastructure

## 6. Digital Public Goods Standard and neutral hosting

The DPG Standard currently emphasizes clear ownership, platform independence, open licensing and open standards. The Linux Foundation describes itself as a neutral home for collaborative open-source development. These are useful readiness precedents for clear asset ownership, open implementation rights, neutral marks and vendor replaceability.

Primary sources:
- https://www.digitalpublicgoods.net/standard
- https://www.linuxfoundation.org/

## 7. Legal characterization of the compact

Phase 13 uses “constitutional compact” as product architecture language. Unless sovereign states or qualifying public bodies create an instrument under applicable law, Tourify must not market it as a treaty, international organization, court, regulator, CMO or governmental registry. The legal effect of ratification, asset locks, fiduciary duties, nonprofit structures, arbitration, governing law, enforceability and public-benefit obligations requires counsel in each relevant jurisdiction.

## 8. Required implementation refresh

2. Confirm entity and fiduciary law in every proposed home jurisdiction.
