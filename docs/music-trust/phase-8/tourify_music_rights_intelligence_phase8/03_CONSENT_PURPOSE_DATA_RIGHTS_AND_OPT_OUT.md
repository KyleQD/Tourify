# Consent, Permitted Purpose, Data Rights and Opt-Out

Consent must be specific enough that an artist can understand which data supports which output and can withdraw future use without corrupting historical audit records.

## Consent dimensions

Subject, controller, asset scope, data categories, permitted purpose, output class, audience, geography, effective date, expiry, retention, withdrawal behavior and whether derivatives already released may remain.

## Purposes

Private diagnostics, anonymized benchmarking, policy research, contract-term education, group-readiness analysis, collective-licensing feasibility, advocacy research and product improvement must be separate purposes rather than one broad checkbox.

## Consent lifecycle

draft → presented → accepted → active → partially withdrawn → expired → revoked. A changed purpose or material methodology requires renewed consent.

## Opt-out propagation

Withdrawal creates a job that removes future raw participation, recalculates affected cohorts, invalidates outputs below thresholds, updates privacy accounting and records whether already published aggregate statistics remain defensible.

## No coercion

Certification, uploads, playback, marketplace access, licensing, rights administration and enforcement cannot depend on Phase 8 consent unless strictly necessary for the requested Phase 8 service.

## Existing-system integration

- Consume versioned Phase 7 events, reconciled official-source mirrors and approved aggregate extracts.
- Never mutate source administration, claim, enforcement, licence, payment or Rights Passport records from an intelligence workflow.
- Keep upload, playback, entitlement, marketplace, profile, feed, EPK, analytics and mobile behavior unchanged unless a separately tested display adapter is added.

## Stop conditions

Stop publication or negotiation activity when consent is invalid, a cohort is too small, one participant dominates the statistic, source data is stale, re-identification risk is unacceptable, competitively sensitive information may be exposed, legal approval is missing, or an output could be understood as a coordinated price or legal instruction.

## Completion evidence

Codex must record audited repository paths, deployed database objects, migrations, RLS tests, route tests, privacy and competition reviews, feature flags, monitoring, rollback instructions and task-level evidence in `phase-8-execution-plan.json`.
